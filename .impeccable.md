# Ekdotico house style — Design Context

This file is the canonical design brief for `/impeccable` runs against
`@ekdotico/design-system`, and for every repository that consumes it. Where a
consuming repository carries its own `.impeccable.md`, this one wins: the
package is the house style, and a second copy that disagrees is the drift the
package exists to end.

> _εκδοτικό_ — "of publishing / editorial." A writer's tool for honest,
> transparent AI-assisted research.

## Design Context

### Users

The house style is worn by three surfaces at once, and it has to hold for all
three without being retuned for any of them.

1. **`docs`** — the design documentation site, read by academic supervisors
   evaluating rigour, by investors assessing risk, and by the implementation
   team treating the artefacts as the contract they build against.
2. **`deree`** — the marketing site and the gated faculty preview, read by
   people who have not used the product and are deciding whether to care.
3. **`praktico`** — an internal research study management tool, used by the
   team itself for long working sessions.

The job to be done is the same underneath all three: **read structured material
without friction**. Reading happens in long sessions, often at a desk,
sometimes printed. Tables, dense lists, code, and prose coexist on one page.
Concentration matters more than novelty.

### Brand Personality

**Three words: scholarly, measured, calm.**

- **Voice**: A research librarian's email — present tense, full sentences, no
  contractions in primary copy, Oxford comma, British spelling. Third person
  for system text ("3 citations require review"); second person only for direct
  instruction ("Select a source to begin."). Never first person plural — "we"
  is the institution, not the software.
- **Casing**: Sentence case throughout. Title Case only for proper nouns and
  the product name.
- **Punctuation**: Em dashes for interruption, en dashes for ranges. Curly
  quotes in body text. Hyphenated compounds ("AI-assisted", "peer-reviewed").
- **Numbers**: Spelled out below ten in prose; numerals in interface text, for
  scanning.
- **Emoji**: Never. Unicode glyphs (§ † ‡ ¶ →) are permitted as section and
  footnote markers.

The interface should evoke **the calm of a well-kept reading room** —
confidence, deliberation, scholarly attention. Never breezy, never gamified,
never excited. The software is a tool, not a mascot.

The full linguistic layer is not summarised here. It is `expression/guidelines.md`
at version 0.3, with the machine-readable vocabulary and prohibitions beside it
and a linter that reads them.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Refined editorial, book design.** Closer to a university press or a
well-bound journal than to a SaaS dashboard.

**Concrete motifs**: ivory parchment surfaces, thin gilt rules separating
sections (a 40px decorative gilt rule over a hairline neutral), a serif reading
column capped at a 72-character measure, marginalia in the side gutter,
page-like surfaces with almost no elevation.

**Theme**: light, and multi-thematic. Four palettes — Parchment as default,
Library, Sepia, Mono — sharing one structural token system across two modes.
Theme is a setting the reader tunes to their working habits. Never
automatically dark, and never dark to look modern.

**Anti-references — what this is not:**

- Not a SaaS dashboard. No gradient backgrounds, no glassmorphism, no glow.
- Not a developer documentation site. No monospace as decoration, no neon
  syntax tokens.
- No emoji illustrations, no coloured left-border accent cards, no dark rounded
  dashboard chrome.
- No marketing photography, no parallax, no bouncy or spring animation.
- No protection gradients. Text sits on solid surfaces.

**Colour:**

- **All colour is OKLCH**, in the token source and in every consumer. Mermaid
  is the single exception: its palette is projected back to hex because its
  theming engine does colour arithmetic on the values it is handed.
- Earth-toned semantics — `--ok` forest green, `--warn` gilt gold, `--danger`
  muted burgundy, `--info` oxford blue. Never the saturated browser defaults.
- Transparency highlights (`--hl-quote`, `--hl-citation`, `--hl-ai`) are
  desaturated washes that stay readable under body text.
- **Accessible values win over the locked brand specification.** `--gilt`
  ships darker than the original brand hex because the original failed WCAG AA
  as small text. Where the two conflict, the specification is amended and the
  contrast is kept.

**Elevation:** every light palette declares its own complete shadow set, and
none inherits another theme's. Elevation reads as light falling on the page, so
its colour comes from the same place the page does — warm brown under
Parchment, green under Library, red-brown under Sepia, neutral under Mono.
Never pure black.

**Typography:**

- **Serif, for reading** — Crimson Pro (`--font-serif`). Page titles, headings,
  blockquotes, anything meant to be read.
- **Sans, for interface** — Commissioner (`--font-sans`). Buttons, menus,
  labels, metadata.
- **Mono, for identifiers** — Fira Code (`--font-mono`). Citation keys,
  document hashes, DOIs, timestamps, code.
- These three are canonical. An earlier specification named Source Serif 4,
  Inter Tight, and JetBrains Mono; the specification was amended to the
  implementation, not the other way round.
- **Reference the token, never the family.** `var(--font-sans)` with no
  fallback list. A fallback that restates a token is a second source of truth
  with nothing keeping it honest.

**Borders and rules**: hairline `1px solid var(--rule)`. Decorative section
breaks use the centred gilt rule. Cards prefer a rule plus a soft shadow over a
hard drop shadow.

**Corner radii**: from the scale — `--radius-sm` for inputs, buttons and menus;
`--radius-lg` for cards and modals; `--radius-xs` for inline code, almost
square, referencing the monospaced tradition; `--radius-pill` for tags and
verification keys.

**Iconography**: Lucide, vendored rather than installed, at 1.5 stroke on a
24×24 viewBox, in one module. Vendoring avoids shipping two thousand glyphs to
serve a dozen; putting them in one module is how the resulting conformance cost
is paid. **Never redraw by eye and never adjust coordinates to taste** — a set
that has been individually tuned stops reading as a set, which is the whole
reason to use one. `check:icons` enforces this against the upstream package.
The Ekdotico mark is the only icon in the system that is not Lucide.

**Motion:**

- Fades at `--dur-base` with `--ease-soft`. No bounce, no spring, no elastic.
- **Transform is permitted only where a surface enters or leaves the viewport**
  — a drawer sliding, a command palette rising. It is prohibited on anything
  already on screen: hover, focus, and state changes fade, they do not move.
  The earlier rule said "fades only", which two long-standing interactions
  already contravened, so it was unenforceable as written.
- Reduced motion shortens durations to `0.01ms` rather than setting
  `transition: none`. The distinction is load-bearing: `none` prevents
  `transitionend` from firing, and components that wait on it stop working.

**Interaction:**

- **The document is the state.** Theme and mode live in `data-theme` and
  `data-mode` on the document element, written by a blocking init script before
  React exists. Components read those attributes through `useSyncExternalStore`
  and never hold a copy — a copy initialised to a default disagrees with the
  page for one frame on every load.
- A control's ARIA role is a promise about the keyboard, and it is kept. A
  `radiogroup` is one tab stop with roving focus, arrows that move and select,
  and Home and End that reach the ends.

### Design Principles

1. **Match the book, not the dashboard.** Every decision pulls toward
   "well-bound journal" and away from "SaaS chrome". If a treatment would look
   at home in a JSTOR PDF, it belongs.
2. **Restraint signals rigour.** The audience evaluates trust through visual
   discipline. Saturated colour, decorative effect, and motion all undermine
   the scholarly contract. Hairlines and warmth over gradients and glow.
3. **Reading is the job.** Prose, code, and tables coexist for long sessions.
   Hierarchy through type and rhythm, not boxes.
4. **Earth tones, never browser defaults.** Semantic states use the
   forest, gilt, burgundy, and oxford-blue palette.
5. **One definition, generated everywhere.** A value that appears twice will
   disagree eventually. Tokens are authored once and every artefact is
   generated; where duplication is unavoidable it is checked, not trusted.
6. **The product name is "Ekdotico"** (Greek _εκδοτικό_, "of publishing").
   Never "Ekdotiko". This is the most common mistake, and it is a blocking
   lint rule.

### Reference files

- `expression/guidelines.md` — the linguistic layer, version 0.3.
- `expression/vocabulary.json`, `expression/prohibited.json` — the same rules,
  machine-readable, which the linter reads and holds no copy of.
- `tokens/source/*.json` — the authored token source. Everything else is
  generated from it.
- `src/styles/base.css` — the base layer: reset, focus ring, skip link,
  screen-reader utility, reduced motion, scrollbars, semantic type utilities.
- `README.md` — installation, subpath exports, and the linter's configuration.
